## Part 2: how to post so it works

Six rules. They matter more than the words.

1. **The first line is the whole post.** LinkedIn shows about 140 characters
   before "see more". If your opening line does not make someone stop, the
   other 200 words never happen.
2. **One idea per post.** Not a feature list. A single thing someone did not
   know.
3. **Short paragraphs, one or two lines each.** A dense block gets scrolled.
4. **Put the link in the post, not the comments.** The old advice about
   comments is out of date, and a link in the comments loses most of the
   clicks.
5. **Three to five hashtags at the end.** Your own tool builds these.
6. **End with a question.** Comments travel much further than likes, and a
   post with ten comments beats one with fifty likes.

**Rhythm:** three posts a week beats seven. Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday
morning, Pakistan time, catches both Europe and the US working day.

**Reality check:** you have 217 followers. Post one reaches 217 people, not
2,000. That is normal and it is not a reason to stop. Reach grows from
comments on other people's posts more than from your own posts, so spend ten
minutes a day leaving real replies on posts by people in SEO, ecommerce and
spreadsheets. That is the actual growth lever early on.

### Post 3 — the SUMIF trap

## Part 4: what to do when someone asks about buying

It will probably not happen this early, and that is fine. If it does:

- **Do not name a price first.** Ask what they have in mind. The first number
  anchors everything.
- **Know your floor.** Sites are valued on monthly net profit, usually 20 to
  40 times it. With no revenue yet, a serious buyer is offering for the
  domain and the code, which is a few hundred dollars, not thousands.
- **Have the numbers ready** before any conversation: monthly visitors from
  Analytics, revenue once AdSense is live, and traffic sources.
- **Six months of revenue data is the unlock.** It is the difference between
  a starter site and a listing on Empire Flippers or Motion Invest.

Keep posting. The audience is the asset that makes the site sellable, and it
takes the same months to build that the tools took days.
